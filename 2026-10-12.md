### Concurrency and real throughput

Added `--concurrency` (ThreadPoolExecutor) to `score_segments_qe.py`,
`score_segments_mt_distributional.py`, and `score_terminology_impact.py`. Benchmarked a sweep
(1/4/8/16) on a small pilot: concurrency past 8 showed diminishing returns (1x -> 1.5x -> 1.8x ->
1.9x speedup) — confirms the bottleneck is CPU compute on this backend, not network latency.
Settled on concurrency 8, matching the 8 physical cores.

### Main calibration (WMT MQM, en-de 2022)

Ran `score_segments_qe.py` and `score_segments_mt_distributional.py` over 1,500 sampled
segments (verified not system-biased: the first 1,500 rows span 5 systems evenly). Each pass
took ~35 minutes; 1,429 and 1,426 segments scored respectively (~4.7-4.9% drop rate — better
than Shindan's own 8-9%). Wrote `scripts/run_main_evaluation.py` (didn't exist yet — only the
terminology arm had an evaluation runner) to join scores with the MQM `score` label and compute
Pearson/Spearman correlation plus confusion-matrix/AUC per metric.

**Result at n=1,426: every metric — plain `mean_logprob` and all seven richer distributional
signals — landed in a 0.49-0.53 AUC band.** `mean_logprob` itself scored 0.534, squarely inside
Shindan's own prior 0.39-0.56 range. This reproduces Shindan's "barely better than chance"
finding independently, and shows the richer, previously-untested distributional signal family
does not fix that weakness either. Full numbers in `FEASIBILITY_REPORT.md` section 4.1.

### Terminology-impact arm: a real surprise

Before running the terminology arm, dry-ran it on the already-downloaded de-en slice and found
90-100% alignment failures (`n_tokens=0`) — a serious problem, not noise. Traced it to
`qe/lib_prompt_logprobs.py`'s marker-detection heuristic breaking down when the model's own
rank-1 guesses drift wildly while "reading" a non-English (German) source (one case's guesses
hallucinated a translation into *Spanish*). Tested en-cs (English-source) pairs on the same
model: 0% alignment failures. Downloaded en-cs and switched the real run to it — documented as a
genuine, model-dependent finding in `TERMINOLOGY_IMPACT.md`, not swept under the rug.

Launched the first real run at 2,500 pairs; after 16 minutes it had only reached 200/2,500 —
extrapolated to 3+ hours, unacceptable for today, so killed it and relaunched at a scoped-down
400 pairs. That run then appeared stuck at 200/400 for ~25 minutes; direct investigation (CPU
time deltas, server request logs) showed it was never actually stalled — the progress counter
only prints every 200 completions, and the server log showed a continuous stream of completed
requests throughout. False alarm, but a real lesson: the scripts' coarse progress reporting
made healthy-but-slow look identical to stuck. Completed: 400 pairs -> 209 usable observations
(65% combined drop rate, disclosed).

**Result: the original hypothesis was not confirmed — the opposite trend appeared, and
significantly.** Forced-term tokens scored *higher* confidence than the rest of the sentence
(mean diff +0.441, Wilcoxon p=1.6e-13), and terminology-constrained translations scored higher
than their paired unconstrained version (mean diff +0.229, p=3.1e-6). Inspecting the actual
required terms explained why: they're common AI/ML technical vocabulary ("static and contextual
multilingual embeddings"), well-represented in the model's training data and fluently integrated
by the competitively-tuned WMT23 systems — not the jarring, unfamiliar client-glossary insertion
the hypothesis imagined. Documented as a real finding with its real limits (doesn't rule out
genuinely novel/rare terms) in `TERMINOLOGY_IMPACT.md` and `FEASIBILITY_REPORT.md`.
