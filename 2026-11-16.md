## PaliVerse RAG — whole-canon pass finished with real numbers, a random-sample trust check, a client citation chain verified, DPPN removed, four new process/planning docs written, and a new client method request assessed — 4 hours (10:30–14:30)

Source: `mini_rag/WORKLOG.MD` and `agent-unit-explanations/WORKLOG.MD`, referred to here as "PaliVerse RAG"

**Whole-canon mechanical pass (launched 11/11, resumed after a mid-run
crash) finished — real final numbers, not the earlier estimate.**
46,466 total real (file_source, chapter_id, unit) combinations — double
the 23,396 figure cited the previous week, which turned out to be
computed from the wrong table/methodology; cross-checked the real total
directly against the database. 232,330 candidate rows: 185,383 GENUINE,
2,606 WEAK-MATCH, 44,341 FLAGGED. Stopped the Azure server immediately
after, since the next phase (batch verification) needs no DB connection.

**Before spending on the full batch, ran a 40-pair random sample across
18 source files to check the mechanical check's logic is actually
trustworthy.** Real result: 34/40 (85%) came back RELATED on live Opus
verification — a much higher rescue rate than chapter 1's 48%, confirming
the mechanical threshold over-flags short verse-form units generally, not
just one book. Manually spot-checked 4 of the 40 against their real text
rather than trusting the label — 3 checked out cleanly, the 4th exposed a
real bug: a non-standard model response was being silently mislabeled as
a confirmed negative. Fixed before it could affect the real 44,341-pair
volume.

**Verified a detailed 9-step citation chain the client supplied**, linking
a known chapter-1 unit to two of the Buddha's chief disciples through the
commentary layers rather than the root text — checked every step directly
against the real corpus rather than taking it on faith. 6 of 9 steps
confirmed with exact textual matches; 2 steps reference a text genuinely
absent from the ingested corpus (confirmed against the full file list,
not just a failed search); 1 likely cites a modern secondary source
outside the project's hierarchy. Wrote up the verification table, what it
means for the existing retrieval design (a different problem — entity/
claim cross-referencing, not passage similarity), and a validation plan
before building anything, in a new research folder.
