not round 1's apparent 0.111-point gap). Revised the plan: run both models at the eventual
1,500-segment scale rather than picking one, since the headline-vs-hard-cut disagreement between
them is itself a real, now-replicated finding, not noise to resolve by picking a winner early.

### Experiments 2 and 3

Held back the planned judge scale-up to finish Experiments 2 and 3 first, on the freshest
existing data (the round-2 200-segment sample). Built shared plumbing (`scripts/openai_common.py`,
`scripts/openai_adapter.py`) both needed.

- **Experiment 2** (generation-mode confidence, `scripts/score_segments_openai_generation.py`):
  had `gpt-4o-mini` translate the 200 round-2 source sentences fresh, scored every
  generation-mode signal against chrF (`sacrebleu`, new dependency) of the generated text vs.
  the WMT reference. **Result: the strongest non-judge signal found anywhere in this project.**
  Plain `mean_logprob` barely moves (r=+0.030), but `mean_margin` (r=+0.446),
  `mean_top_entropy` (r=-0.428), `weak_share` (r=-0.344), and `composite_score` (r=+0.233) all
  show real, moderate correlation with quality. Caveat flagged prominently: checked against
  chrF (a proxy), not a real human label — a promising lead needing a human-labeled follow-up,
  not yet equal evidence to Experiment 1's judge result.
- **Experiment 3** (self-consistency/agreement, `scripts/score_segments_openai_agreement.py`):
  generated N=4 samples per source at temperature=0.8, computed `agreement_score()` verbatim,
  scored against mean chrF and against the real human MQM label. **Result: null both ways**
  (r=-0.053 vs. chrF, r=+0.111 vs. the human label). Self-consistency doesn't track quality here.
