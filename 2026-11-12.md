## LogPrompts Research (Alpha-Log-Probs) — Format-lead investigation, a 3-paper verbalized-confidence comparison, judge-as-filter and combined-score analysis — 4 hours (15:30–19:30)

Source: pulled from the `Alpha-Log-Probs` GitHub repo (`worklog.md`).

Aim: look at why the Format judge reached a headline AUC of 0.742, compare a specific
published verbalized-confidence method against the project's own P(Yes) judge on the same
segments (per a management email citing three papers), and write the conclusion of the judge
experiment.

**Corrected a real reporting error before anything else**: the figures 0.853 (Format) and 0.712
(Completeness) quoted earlier were the upper ends of 95% confidence intervals, not the actual
scores — clarified before any further analysis built on the wrong numbers.

**Investigated the Format lead directly, not just reported it.** Checked where the 0.742 AUC
held up and where it didn't across the four language pairs: held in en-ru and en-de, did not
hold in en-es, where real and false flags make identical claims in the text. The specific
error-type patterns behind the lead did not replicate between pairs either. Treated as a
hypothesis only, not a confirmed finding, since no new Format flags exist to test it further.

**Read the three papers management cited** (Servajean et al. on verbalized confidence, BACON on
budgeted human calibration, Pong on post-hoc correction), then fetched the verbalized-confidence
paper's actual prompt from the authors' own public GitHub repository after getting the user's
explicit yes — treated as untrusted data and never executed as code, copied verbatim under its
MIT licence, left unchanged.

**Built `scripts/p17_verbalized_confidence.py`** — runs that paper's own prompt against the
Completeness and Format test rows (severity + confidence), with `--smoke`, `--run`, and
`--analyse` modes, and compares the result directly against the project's existing P(Yes) judge
score.
- **Real result**: verbalized confidence is **weaker than P(Yes) in both categories tested** —
  Completeness's confidence-alone score (0.553) sits well below P(Yes)'s 0.649, and Format's
  (0.471) well below its 0.742, with every comparison's confidence interval landing on the
  negative side. The published method doesn't improve on this project's own simpler approach.
- **Real bug found and fixed before trusting any of these numbers**: a cache-key bug (using the
  wrong model name as the key) made P(Yes) scores come up missing on the first run — fixed and
  rerun on the already-cached model responses (no new API cost) before reading any result,
  disclosed in the write-up rather than silently corrected.

**Built `scripts/p17_judge_conclusion.py`** — two further analyses to close out the judge
experiment: judge-as-a-filter (a cut-off fitted on training+validation data to lose at most 15%
of real flags, then applied once to the test half) and a combined score (log-probability filter
plus judge, evaluated via grouped 5-fold cross-validation repeated 10 times).
- **Real result, judge as filter**: a real, useful effect in 5 of 7 categories — e.g. Format
  removes 66.7% of false flags for 39.6% of real flags lost (a real advantage over a random drop
  of the same size, +0.271), Completeness and Punctuation and Meaning all show smaller but real
  advantages too. Glossary is the one category where the filter is actually worse than random
  (-0.016).
- **Real result, combined score**: combining the log-probability filter and the judge score
  adds nothing beyond noise in either category tested (Completeness, Format) — the combined
  AUCs land within the same confidence interval as either signal alone.

**Wrote the conclusion of the judge experiment directly into the project's calibration and
tuning-log documents.** Overall: the judge is a real but modest signal, similar in size to the
log-probability filters, in Completeness, Meaning, and Punctuation (weaker in Style and
Grammar); it fails the pre-registered pass/fail rule in 6 of the 7 categories tested (Meaning
passes, but only narrowly — its confidence interval's lower bound is +0.001). Verbalized
confidence from the cited paper is weaker than this project's own P(Yes) approach. Combining
signals adds nothing. None of the three cited papers improved on this project's existing
results; BACON's cross-judge-variance idea specifically was not tested.

**Caveats disclosed plainly, not glossed over**: Format's real test sample is only 96 rows;
clustering of real flags lowers the effective sample size well below the raw row count; no
Holm correction was applied across the judge tests (an open methodological gap, stated
outright); and the cache-key bug above is recorded in the write-up as something that happened
and was caught, not quietly fixed and forgotten.
